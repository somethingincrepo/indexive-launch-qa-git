<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Verify a published page by reading the public URL back against the copy and settings you approved before publishing, since the CMS view can differ from what the public sees. Confirm the document is published, load the public page logged out, compare the copy, then check the title, structured data, accessibility basics, indexing intent and canonical signals.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Prepared 2026-11-03 by the Indexive QA editorial team. Saved draft text, published document state and the public page are separate things, so each check below looks at one signal and keeps it separate from the others. Where an example helps, we use one explicitly fictional page, the Northstar Lab QA demonstration.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What you need before you publish</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>You need a baseline recorded before anything goes live: the approved copy, the title, the meta text, the canonical URL and your intent for indexing. Without it, the readback has nothing to compare against and you end up judging the page by feel.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The approved copy is what you compare the public headline and body against. You hold the title up against the guidance on distinct, descriptive and accurate titles. Meta text gives you something to compare line by line on the public page. Your canonical signals all have to agree with the recorded canonical URL. Your indexing intent, noindex or indexable, tells you whether a directive in the source is a pass or a failure. If you record only some of these, the checks tied to the missing items have nothing to compare against.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Write the baseline down before you publish, because a baseline reconstructed afterward from memory or from the live page can't be trusted. A baseline copied from the live page can only confirm that the page matches itself.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>You also need a logged-out browser window, so you see what the public sees, and a way to view the page source. Our fictional Northstar Lab QA demonstration page is the example throughout. It carries a title, a meta description, a canonical URL, WebPage JSON-LD and an intentional noindex,nofollow. It is a labelled synthetic example and has no connection to a real business or a case study. For a worksheet to record the baseline, see <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-baseline-capture">Publishing Review Baseline Capture: Copy and Canonical URL</a>.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Steps to verify a published page</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Work in this order. Saving a draft, publishing, and checking the public result are three different events, and each step below confirms one of them.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Confirm the document is published, not just saved</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Check the document ID in your Sanity Studio or API. According to <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">Sanity's drafts and versions documentation</a>, drafts carry a drafts. ID prefix and published documents have no such prefix. The published perspective excludes unpublished changes, and later draft edits do not alter the published document until you publish.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Don't treat matching text or a recent updatedAt value as proof of anything about the publishing process. Per <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">the same Sanity documentation</a>, current text or updatedAt alone does not prove that publication never occurred, and direct writes to a published document can also change it. If you don't know how your frontend queries content, don't assume it uses the published perspective. Our fictional QA reference page, <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-published-document">Sanity Published Document and Public QA Renderer</a>, distinguishes published-perspective document state from the separate public renderer observation.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Load the public URL and compare it to the approved copy</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Open the public URL in the logged-out window and compare the headline, body and meta text with your baseline, line by line. A 200 status is not the test. <a href="https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Status/200">MDN's documentation on HTTP 200</a> says it indicates request success, and a status alone does not compare the delivered copy with an approved draft.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Record any difference, even a small one, as a failure of this step. A changed word in the headline, a missing paragraph or different meta text each means the public page does not match what was approved. Note exactly where it differs so you can decide later whether to fix forward or revert. For a worksheet to log the readback, see <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-public-readback">Publishing Review Public Readback: Published Copy and Canonical URL</a>.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Check the title element and structured data</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Read the title in the page source. <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link">Google recommends</a> titles that are distinct, descriptive, concise and accurate about the page. Then open the JSON-LD. <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data">Google's structured data documentation</a> says markup gives explicit information about a page and should represent the visible page content.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>In the fictional Northstar Lab example, the WebPage JSON-LD text should match what the page displays. If the markup says one thing and the visible page says another, fix the markup or the copy so they agree.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>If the title fails, check whether it is vague, overlong or no longer describes the page after an edit. Rewrite it against the same guidance, republish, and read the source again. Check the title in the page source of the public page, not in the editing screen.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Run the preliminary accessibility checks</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Run through the <a href="https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/">W3C Easy Checks</a>: page title, alternative text, headings, contrast and keyboard access. They are preliminary checks; passing them does not establish comprehensive accessibility conformance.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Treat a pass as a screening result only. W3C states that passing these checks is not comprehensive accessibility conformance proof, so don't report the page as conformant.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>If one of the checks fails, for example the page has no usable title or an image lacks alternative text, fix that issue and run the same check again. Record the result as a preliminary check in your notes, so nobody later reads it as a conformance statement.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Check indexing intent and canonical signals</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>First confirm the indexing state matches your intent. A noindex meta tag or response header can prevent Google indexing when the crawler can access the page, but <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing">Google notes</a> that blocking crawling in robots.txt prevents Google from seeing that rule. The fictional Northstar Lab page is deliberately noindex,nofollow, so the correct result there is that the directive is present. Confirm it, and don't remove it.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Next, check canonical consistency. <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls">Google treats rel=canonical and redirects as strong signals</a> and sitemap inclusion as weaker, and your preferences should agree with each other. Your preferred URL is also distinct from the canonical Google selects, as <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization">Google's canonicalization documentation</a> explains.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Finally, don't mistake a canonical for a redirect. A <a href="https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Status/301">301 response</a> means the resource moved permanently, with the destination in the Location header, and a browser requests that destination. Because a canonical link implies no redirect, you should test redirect behavior separately.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>If the indexing check fails, compare the directive in the source with your recorded intent. A missing noindex on a page meant to be noindex, or a noindex you did not intend, means correcting the directive. If robots.txt blocks the page, Google can't see the rule. When canonical signals disagree, for instance the canonical points to one URL and a redirect or sitemap entry points to another, align them so they name the same preferred URL.</p>

<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What to do when a check fails</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Write down which check failed and what the baseline said it should be. Compare the current public content and canonical value with the intended prior revision, then decide whether to fix forward or revert. Our worksheet <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-rollback-decision">Publishing Review Rollback Decision: Prior Copy and Canonical URL</a> is a navigation reference for that decision; we don't claim a specific rollback method here.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Keep the record of what passed separate from any measured outcomes. Passing these checks does not prove indexing, accessibility conformance or results, and this page offers no outcome data.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>A useful record lists each check, the baseline value, what you observed on the public page, and whether it passed. Keeping the baseline value beside the observation shows what the page should say when you decide whether to revert.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>
<li>Treating a 200 status as correct content. A 200 indicates request success only, and a status alone does not compare the delivered copy with the approved draft.</li>
<li>Trusting CMS text or updatedAt as proof of publication. Direct writes can change a published document, so neither value settles it. Check the document ID and the public page.</li>
<li>Blocking a noindex page in robots.txt. Google then cannot see the noindex rule on that page.</li>
<li>Assuming a canonical link redirects. A canonical declares a preferred URL. A 301 response redirects the browser to its Location destination. Test redirects on their own.</li>
<li>Calling Easy Checks conformance. They are preliminary only, and W3C says passing them is not comprehensive conformance proof.</li>
</ul>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Does a 200 response mean the page is correct?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>No. It means the request succeeded. You still have to compare the delivered copy with the approved draft.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Does updatedAt prove publication happened?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>No. Current text or updatedAt alone doesn't prove it, and direct writes can change a published document.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Is a noindex page a failure?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Not if you intended it. Confirm the directive is present and not hidden by a robots.txt block.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Is a canonical the same as a redirect?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>No. A canonical declares a preferred URL, while a 301 sends the browser to a Location destination.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Do Easy Checks prove accessibility?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>No. They are preliminary checks and not comprehensive conformance proof.</p>

<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>By Indexive QA editorial team. Dated 2026-11-03. The Northstar Demo Lab scenario material here, including the editor Casey and every worksheet, is fictional and was defined on 2026-11-02.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Editing a Sanity draft leaves the published document untouched until publication, so the public page can lag behind the editor. To confirm that a published change reached a public page, record the expected copy and URL first, publish, then read the public page back and compare. We don't claim any connector API, authentication setup or partnership between Sanity and a renderer, because our evidence doesn't establish one.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What the published document and the public renderer each hold</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>According to <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">Sanity's documentation on drafts and versions</a>, drafts have a drafts. ID prefix and published documents have none. The published perspective excludes unpublished changes, and later draft edits don't alter the published document until publication. The drafts perspective applies drafts for preview.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The public renderer is a separate layer. Our evidence doesn't state its query or delivery configuration, so you check what it actually serves rather than guessing. Our page, <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-published-document">Sanity Published Document and Public QA Renderer</a>, draws the same line between document state and renderer observation.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What you need before you start</h2>
<ul style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>
<li>A draft baseline: the copy you expect to see after publishing.</li>
<li>An expected public URL where that copy should appear.</li>
<li>A checklist, and a way to record each result as pass, fail or unknown.</li>
<li>A named, responsible reviewer for anything left unresolved.</li>
</ul>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The routine belongs to Casey, a fictional editor persona who is neither a real customer nor an expert. For a practice target, our <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/guide">public QA guide</a> is a disposable, explicitly fictional page marked noindex,nofollow. It has a title, description, canonical and WebPage JSON-LD, and states a workflow of baseline, change, publish, public verification.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>How to verify a published change step by step</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Each step below has one input and one output. The worksheets are scenario specifications only.</p>
<ol style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>
<li>Record the baseline. Input: expected copy and expected public URL. Output: a saved Baseline Capture.</li>
<li>Change the draft. Make your edit to the draft only. Output: an edited draft, with the published document still unchanged.</li>
<li>Publish the draft. Output: a changed published document. Don't treat the editor's preview as proof of this step.</li>
<li>Read the public page back. Input: the expected URL. Output: the observed response and content, recorded as a Public Readback.</li>
<li>Compare and record. Match observed values against the baseline and write pass, fail or unknown. Send unresolved items to your reviewer.</li>
</ol>

<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Record the baseline</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Write down the exact copy and the exact URL before you touch anything. Without this, you have nothing to compare against. Our fictional worksheet page, <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-public-readback">Publishing Review Public Readback: Published Copy and Canonical URL</a>, is the scenario specification for the readback side of this routine.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Read the public page back</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>After publishing, fetch the expected URL and capture what comes back. Compare it with the baseline and record pass, fail or unknown.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What to do when the public page still shows old copy</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>First, don't conclude from the document alone. Per the same Sanity documentation, the published document stays intact while a draft is edited, and direct writes can also change it. Current document text or an updatedAt timestamp therefore can't prove that publication never occurred.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Second, don't use preview as evidence. The drafts perspective applies drafts, so it shows what the public page may not. A public frontend using the published perspective excludes draft-only edits, but if you don't know the frontend's query or delivery configuration, don't assume it. Record unknown, not pass, and refer it to the reviewer.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>How to roll back a bad publish</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Use a Rollback Decision: record the reason, the prior revision and a post-restore check. Restoring the document isn't confirmation by itself, so read the public page back again; this is a procedural recommendation drawn from the scenario worksheet. In a controlled QA experiment, rollback was run on 17 synthetic software fixtures, with file absence and a public 404 recorded. That is an example of a verification process, not proof of general capability.</p>
<ol style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>
<li>Write down why the publish is being rolled back.</li>
<li>Identify the prior revision you intend to restore.</li>
<li>Restore that revision.</li>
<li>Read the expected public URL back and compare it with the prior revision you intended to restore.</li>
<li>Record pass, fail or unknown, and send anything unresolved to your reviewer.</li>
</ol>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What a QA run proves and does not prove</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Those 17 fixtures were deterministic software tests, not 17 customers or 17 AI-written articles. They aren't production, search-indexing or accessibility proof, and they can't establish failure rates. The fixture receipts do not establish search indexing. The guide is intentionally noindex,nofollow; that directive is not a measurement of its actual index status. The guide does carry a title, description, canonical and WebPage JSON-LD, so those are the elements a readback can record. The scenario stays fictional throughout.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>FAQ</h2>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Why does the public page still show old copy?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The edit may still be an unpublished draft, which the published perspective excludes. Or the renderer may be reading something else, and the evidence doesn't say how it is configured. Read the public URL back and record what you see.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Does updatedAt prove a document was never published?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>No. Direct writes can change the published document, so current text or updatedAt alone can't show that publication never occurred.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What if the frontend configuration is unknown?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Record unknown and refer it to a responsible reviewer. Don't assume the renderer uses the published perspective.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Is a restored document enough after a rollback?</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>No. Record the reason and prior revision, then run a post-restore check on the public page.</p>

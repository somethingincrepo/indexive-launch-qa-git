<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>By the Indexive QA editorial team. Article date: 2026-11-03. Scenario definitions dated 2026-11-02.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Casey, an explicitly fictional editor, checks a published page by comparing it with an approved draft baseline at an expected public URL, then recording pass, fail or unknown for each check. Casey is a made-up persona and doesn't stand in for a customer or an expert byline.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Everything about Casey, the worksheets and the priced options below is a fictional test scenario. No purchase or contact action is available.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What Casey needs to get done</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Casey's job starts after an approved draft goes live: confirm that the public page matches what was approved. Casey begins with a draft baseline, picks the expected public URL, and runs a checklist against the live page.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Each check ends as pass, fail or unknown. Anything unresolved goes to a responsible reviewer instead of being guessed at.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Why an HTTP 200 does not confirm the approved copy</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>HTTP 200 only indicates that a request succeeded. According to <a href="https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Status/200">MDN's documentation on the 200 status</a>, a response status alone does not compare the delivered copy with an approved draft. A page can return 200 and still show the wrong text.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>So Casey compares content. The fictional worksheet specification <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-baseline-capture">Publishing Review Baseline Capture: Copy and Canonical URL</a> is specified to record the expected copy and URL. A second fictional worksheet, <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-public-readback">Publishing Review Public Readback: Published Copy and Canonical URL</a>, records the observed response and content. Casey sets one beside the other and notes every difference.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Can a Sanity document that looks unchanged prove nothing was published?</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>No. Per <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">Sanity's drafts and versions documentation</a>, drafts carry a drafts. ID prefix and published documents don't. The published perspective excludes unpublished changes, and later draft edits leave the published document intact until publication.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The same documentation says direct writes to the published document can also change it. That means current document text or an updatedAt value alone doesn't prove publication never happened. Casey also can't assume anything about an unknown frontend's query or delivery configuration, so document state and what a public page displays are checked separately.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Page checks that compare delivered content with the draft</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}><a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link">Google's title link guidance</a> recommends titles that are distinct, descriptive and concise and that accurately describe the page, so Casey checks each of those. Structured data (JSON-LD) should represent the visible content, per <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data">Google's structured data introduction</a>, so Casey compares the markup with the text on the page.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Indexability needs care. A noindex meta tag or header can prevent indexing only when the crawler can access it, and blocking crawling in robots.txt stops Google from seeing that rule, according to <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing">Google's guidance on blocking indexing</a>. For duplicates, <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls">Google's documentation on consolidating duplicate URLs</a> treats rel=canonical and redirects as strong signals and sitemap inclusion as weaker, and says canonical preferences should be consistent.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>For preliminary accessibility, Casey can run the <a href="https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/">W3C Easy Checks</a>: titles, alternative text, headings, contrast and keyboard access. Passing them is not comprehensive accessibility conformance proof.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Recording unknown results and who receives them</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>When a check can't be completed, Casey records unknown instead of forcing a pass or fail. The unresolved item then goes to a responsible reviewer. Casey keeps unknown separate from pass and fail, so an unresolved item is not mistaken for a result.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What a rollback record needs</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The fictional <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-rollback-decision">Publishing Review Rollback Decision: Prior Copy and Canonical URL</a> worksheet records a reason, the prior revision and a post-restore check. In the disposable fixtures, rollback was verified by file absence and a public 404.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What the disposable fixture checks showed</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>A controlled QA experiment ran 17 deterministic synthetic page-type fixtures. Every one had persisted verification, a duplicate retry without a provider SHA change, and rollback verified by file absence and public 404.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The 17 fixtures are software tests, and none of them is a customer or an AI-written article. They don't prove production behavior, search indexing or accessibility, and they can't establish general failure rates. The public QA guide is a disposable, fictional, noindex,nofollow page with a title, description, canonical and WebPage JSON-LD, and it states a baseline, change, publish, verification workflow.</p>

<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Getting started with a publishing review</h2>
<ol style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>
<li>Capture the expected copy and URL in the Baseline Capture worksheet. Separately, record the approved title and metadata for comparison.</li>
<li>Choose the expected public URL, so every later comparison points at one named page.</li>
<li>Read back the public page and compare copy, title, markup and canonical against the baseline, noting the observed response and content as a Public Readback worksheet does.</li>
<li>Record pass, fail or unknown for each check, and send unknown items to a responsible reviewer.</li>
<li>If a check fails, decide whether to roll back, recording the reason, the prior revision and a post-restore check.</li>
</ol>

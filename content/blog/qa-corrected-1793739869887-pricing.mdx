<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What drives the price from Basic to Team</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The step from Basic to Extended is about scope. Page count rises from one to three, and the review adds structured data consistency, canonical and indexability checks, preliminary keyboard, heading and image checks, and a rollback decision log.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The step from Extended to Team is about coordination. Team covers ten pages with the same Extended checklist, then adds a shared sign-off sheet and two reviewer roles. Team adds review coordination and no further technical guarantees, so the higher price does not mean deeper technical checking.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The per-exercise prices cover different page counts: one page for USD49, three pages for USD149 and ten pages for USD299. These prices are authored test data, not market research.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What each review checks and why</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The checks exist because a published page can differ from the approved draft in ways a quick glance misses. A public URL check compares the delivered copy itself, since, as MDN documents, <a href="https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Status/200">an HTTP 200 response indicates request success only</a>. A 200 status says nothing about whether the delivered page matches the approved draft.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Draft state matters too. Sanity documents that <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">drafts carry a drafts. ID prefix and the published perspective excludes unpublished changes</a>. Sanity notes that <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">document text or updatedAt alone does not prove publication never occurred</a>, so the CMS record alone cannot settle what the public page shows. The Public Readback worksheet idea is described on <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-public-readback">Publishing Review Public Readback: Published Copy and Canonical URL</a>.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Extended adds indexability and canonical checks. Google explains that <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing">a noindex rule can only be seen if the crawler can access the page</a>, so blocking crawling in robots.txt hides that rule. On canonicals, Google describes <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls">rel=canonical and redirects as strong canonical signals and sitemaps as weaker</a>, which is why canonical preferences should agree.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Title, metadata and structured data</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Basic compares titles and metadata. Google recommends <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link">distinct, descriptive, concise titles that accurately describe the page</a>. The comparison checks consistency and makes no ranking claim.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Extended checks JSON-LD, a format for structured data, against the page. Google says <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data">markup should represent the visible page content</a>. We promise no rich results or rankings from this.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What a review does not cover</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Basic does not include code changes, accessibility certification, SEO ranking guarantees or recurring monitoring. Extended does not certify WCAG conformance or fix production systems. Team adds review coordination rather than additional technical guarantees; its definition does not separately specify an exclusion list.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Each fictional tier lists a review scope. Basic explicitly excludes code changes, accessibility certification, SEO ranking guarantees and recurring monitoring. Extended explicitly excludes WCAG conformance certification and production fixes. Team supplies the Extended checklist with coordination and no additional technical guarantees; do not infer other exclusions that its definition does not state.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The preliminary keyboard, heading and image checks are a first pass. According to the W3C, <a href="https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/">passing preliminary Easy Checks is not comprehensive accessibility conformance proof</a>. A rollback record, such as the fictional worksheet <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-rollback-decision">Publishing Review Rollback Decision: Prior Copy and Canonical URL</a>, holds a reason, a prior revision and a post-restore check.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>When a self-managed review is enough</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>A self-managed review uses a browser and your own checklist. No price is assigned. You supply the time, the expertise and a written record of what you observe. No competing vendor is named for this route.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The fictional editor Casey, an illustrative persona and not a customer, works in sequence: capture a draft baseline, pick the expected public URL, run the checklist, record pass, fail or unknown, and refer unresolved issues to a responsible reviewer. For the Baseline Capture worksheet idea, see <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-baseline-capture">Publishing Review Baseline Capture: Copy and Canonical URL</a>.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The scenario defines the self-managed route only as a fourth option: a browser, an editor-owned checklist and no assigned software purchase price. The evidence does not establish that a solo editor's own written record is enough, so treat that as your own judgment call. Team's shared sign-off sheet and two reviewer roles add coordination for reviews that involve more than one person.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The self-managed route has real requirements even with no price assigned. You need time to run the checklist on each page, enough expertise to read the results, and a record of what you observed, including the items you marked unknown. Recording an item as unknown keeps the doubt visible, so unresolved issues can be referred to a responsible reviewer.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The three worksheet concepts cover the record. Baseline Capture records expected copy and URL, Public Readback records the observed response and content, and Rollback Decision records a reason, a prior revision and a post-restore check. The worksheets are scenario specifications. A supplied specification-page URL is not evidence that a downloadable worksheet exists.</p>

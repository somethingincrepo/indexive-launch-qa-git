<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Why a 200 response does not prove the approved copy is live</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>A 200 status means the request succeeded. According to <a href="https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Status/200">MDN's documentation of HTTP 200</a>, a response status alone does not compare the delivered copy with an approved draft. A page can load cleanly and still show the wrong text.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Document state is a separate question. In Sanity, drafts carry a drafts. ID prefix and published documents do not, and the published perspective excludes unpublished changes, so later draft edits leave the published document unchanged until publication <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">per Sanity's drafts and versions documentation</a>. Direct writes can also change a published document, so current text or an updatedAt value alone does not prove publication never happened. A frontend's query or delivery configuration should not be assumed either <a href="https://www.sanity.io/docs/content-lake/drafts-and-versions">Sanity documentation</a>.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>That is why both options include a public URL check against approved copy. The public URL check observes what the public page actually returns, which neither a status code nor document state can show. A baseline record of expected copy and URL, like the one in our <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-baseline-capture">Publishing Review Baseline Capture worksheet</a>, gives the check something to compare against, and the <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-public-readback">Publishing Review Public Readback page</a> covers the observed side. Both are fictional specifications.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>What Extended adds and what it still cannot prove</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Extended adds four checks. Each one can change a decision, and each one stops short of a guarantee.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Structured data comes first. Google says structured data should represent the visible page content <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data">in its structured data introduction</a>. JSON-LD that matches the draft but not the public page would fail that test, which is why Extended checks JSON-LD against content.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Indexability is next. A noindex tag or header can keep a page out of Google when the crawler can reach it, but blocking crawling in robots.txt stops Google from seeing that rule <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing">according to Google's block-indexing documentation</a>. Checking both together avoids trusting a noindex that nobody can read.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Canonical checks follow. Google treats rel=canonical and redirects as strong signals and sitemap inclusion as weaker, and canonical preferences should be consistent <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls">per its duplicate-URL guidance</a>. A sitemap entry alone does not settle which URL wins.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Last are the preliminary keyboard, heading and image checks. The preliminary checks resemble the W3C Easy Checks, and W3C states that passing them is not comprehensive accessibility conformance proof <a href="https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/">W3C preliminary checks</a>. Extended does not certify WCAG conformance.</p>
<h3 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Rollback decision log</h3>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The rollback decision records a reason, a prior revision and a post-restore check. Extended includes it and Basic does not. After a bad publish, the log shows why the restore happened, what was restored and whether the restore was confirmed. Our fictional <a href="https://scoring-spell-mailing-lenses.trycloudflare.com/qa-semantic-rollback-decision">Publishing Review Rollback Decision page</a> carries the worksheet.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>In a controlled test, 17 synthetic page-type fixtures each ran a rollback with file absence and a public 404. The 17 fixtures are a software test sample and cannot establish general failure rates.</p>
<h2 style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>When Basic is enough and when it is not</h2>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Basic is enough for one page where the worry is copy, title, metadata or the public URL. It is not enough if you need structured data, canonical, accessibility or rollback records, because those checks are not listed in its defined scope.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>A self-managed review has no software price, using a browser and your own checklist. The cost is your time, expertise and a record of observations. It is not a named competing vendor, just the fourth option in the scenario.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>A fictional editor, Casey, shows how this works in practice. Casey starts with a draft baseline, selects the expected public URL and runs a checklist. Each item is recorded as pass, fail or unknown, and anything unresolved goes to a responsible reviewer. Casey is a fictional persona, not a real customer or expert. This route asks the editor to supply time, expertise and a record of observations, and the scenario does not specify a separate deliverable beyond the editor's record of observations.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Extended fits up to three pages with technical checks. The deciding questions are simple. Does the page carry JSON-LD that must match what visitors see? Do the noindex directive and canonical signals match the intended indexing settings and preferred URL? Would a bad publish need a recorded restore? If any answer is yes, Basic leaves that risk unchecked, because those checks are not listed in its defined scope. Basic covers the case where the only worry is whether the approved wording, title and metadata are public, and it costs less.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Past three pages, Team at an illustrative USD299 covers ten pages with the Extended checklist, a shared sign-off sheet and two reviewer roles. Team adds review coordination, not additional technical guarantees, so it changes how many pages and reviewer roles are covered rather than how deeply each page is checked.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Each option has limits. Basic is narrow, Extended covers three pages and does not certify WCAG conformance, and Team costs more while adding coordination rather than additional technical guarantees. All three are our own fictional definitions.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>Honest fit matters here because we are one of the options. Basic is the wrong choice if you need JSON-LD consistency or canonical and indexability checks, because they are not listed in its defined scope. Extended is the wrong choice if you need accessibility certification, since it runs only preliminary checks, or if you expect production fixes, since its definition excludes fixing production systems. The self-managed route is the wrong choice if nobody has time to keep a record of observations.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>The scenario also treats Region A and Region B as remote service areas. There is no office, address, phone number, travel time or local coverage claim behind them, so they should not influence the choice between options.</p>
<p style={{"maxWidth":"100%","overflowWrap":"anywhere","boxSizing":"border-box"}}>One practical test helps when the choice is close: write down what you would do if a check came back as unknown. If the answer is to send it to a reviewer, the scope of Basic may suffice. Choose Extended when that unresolved item concerns one of its listed structured-data, canonical or indexability checks.</p>
